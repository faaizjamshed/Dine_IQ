1.1 Background and Necessity for the Application

Restaurant businesses generate large volumes of data through customer orders,
menu items, pricing, ratings, promotions, inventory consumption, food wastage,
ordering channels, and restaurant locations. Analyzing this information is
essential for identifying profitable dishes, understanding customer preferences,
reducing wastage, improving pricing decisions, and increasing operational
efficiency.
Traditional restaurant analysis often depends on basic reports, spreadsheets, or
manual comparison of sales figures. These methods may not reveal complex
relationships between menu performance, profitability, customer behavior,
promotions, ratings, wastage, locations, and time-based ordering patterns. The
DineIQ Analytics application aims to address this limitation through Big Data
processing, Data Science, and predictive analytics.
The `DineIQ Analytics' application automatically analyzes large restaurant
datasets to identify profitable items, slow-moving dishes, peak ordering periods,
and customer segments. It also identifies frequently purchased combinations,
wastage patterns, price sensitivity, promotion effectiveness, and unusual
transaction behavior. It also predicts future demand and provides data-driven
recommendations for menu optimization, inventory planning, pricing,
promotions, and customer targeting.
The goal is to deliver a scalable, accurate, and intelligent solution that enables
restaurant managers and analysts to make faster and more reliable decisions
based on large volumes of data.

1.2 Proposed Solution

The proposed solution introduces a Big Data and Data Science-powered, Web-
based restaurant intelligence platform that analyzes large volumes of restaurant
transaction and operational data.
The application collects and processes data related to orders, menu items,
customers, restaurant locations, pricing, ratings, promotions, inventory
consumption, and food wastage. Apache Spark, PySpark, and Spark SQL are
used to process the large datasets, perform transformations, create analytical
features, and generate large-scale restaurant insights.
The application analyzes every menu item using multiple indicators such as
sales volume, revenue, cost, contribution margin, ratings, repeat purchases,
wastage, promotion dependency, and demand trends. Based on these indicators,
menu items are classified into categories such as Profit Driver, Volume Driver,
Hidden Opportunity, and Low Performer.

The application also performs customer segmentation, market-basket analysis,
peak-period analysis, demand forecasting, price sensitivity analysis, promotion
effectiveness analysis, wastage prediction, location comparison, and anomaly
detection.

                                                                           © Aptech Limited
To make the analytical results independently verifiable, teams must develop two
separate Data Science pipelines:

    1. Big Data Pipeline: Apache Spark, PySpark, Spark SQL, and Spark MLlib
    2. Python Data Science Pipeline: Python, Pandas, Scikit-learn, XGBoost,

         or other suitable Python Machine Learning libraries
Both pipelines must independently solve selected analytical problems using the
same underlying restaurant dataset. The application must compare their
predictions or classifications and identify matches, differences, and
inconsistencies.
The final Web dashboard must present business insights, predictions, alerts,
menu classifications, customer segments, forecasts, and actionable
recommendations.

Step 10: Menu Performance Classification

Each menu item must be classified into one of the following categories:

Profit Driver

High demand and high profitability with acceptable wastage.

Volume Driver

High demand but comparatively lower profitability.

sales.

Low Performer

Weak demand, weak profitability, excessive wastage, poor ratings, or an
unfavorable combination of indicators.

The classification must be data-driven and must not depend on a single hard-

1.3 Purpose of the Document

The purpose of this document is to outline the design, functionality, and
implementation plan of the proposed DineIQ Analytics application. This
document ensures a shared understanding of the project objectives and provides
a structured roadmap for the development, testing, and deployment of the
application. It is intended for project stakeholders, developers, evaluators,
restaurant managers, data analysts, and administrators.

1.4 Scope of Project

The scope of this project is to design and implement a Big Data and Data Science-
powered, Web-based DineIQ Analytics application. The application assists
restaurant managers, analysts, and administrators in analyzing large volumes
of restaurant data. The application will process order, menu, pricing, customer,
rating, promotion, inventory, and wastage datasets using Apache Spark,
PySpark, Spark SQL, and Machine Learning techniques to identify profitable

                                                                           © Aptech Limited
items, slow-moving dishes, customer segments, peak periods, ordering patterns,
wastage risks, and demand trends. It aims to reduce manual analysis, improve
decision-making, support menu and pricing optimization, and generate
actionable recommendations based on restaurant performance data. Integration
with live POS systems, payment gateways, third-party delivery platforms, or
enterprise restaurant management applications falls outside the scope of this
project.

1.5 Constraints

The DineIQ Analytics project faces certain constraints such as dependency on
quality, completeness, consistency, and accuracy of the order, menu, customer,
pricing, rating, promotion, inventory, and wastage datasets. The application's
effectiveness is limited by the size, realism, balance, and reliability of the
generated dataset as well as the available computational resources for Big Data
processing. Differences may also occur between the analytical results or
predictions generated by the Apache Spark/PySpark pipeline and the
independent Python Data Science pipeline. Privacy and security concerns
regarding the storage and handling of customer, transaction, pricing, and
restaurant business data must also be addressed in accordance with applicable
data protection regulations.

1.7 Non-Functional Requirements

There are several non-functional requirements that should be fulfilled by the application
which are as follows:

    1. Performance: The application should process uploaded claim details and
         generate predictions from both models within five seconds under normal
         operating conditions.

    2. Scalable: The architecture should support increasing the dataset to at
         least five million order-line records without requiring a complete redesign.

    3. Usable: The application should provide an intuitive and user-friendly Web
         interface for restaurant managers, analysts, regional managers, and
         administrators.

    4. Accuracy: Classification models should target at least 85% test accuracy
         or a macro F1-score of at least 0.80 where appropriate. Forecasting models
         must demonstrate measurable improvement over a simple baseline
         forecast.

   5. Available: The application should remain operational during required

         evaluation periods with at least 99% uptime under normal operating
         conditions.

                   These are the bare minimum expectations from the project. It is a must
                   to implement the FUNCTIONAL and NON-FUNCTIONAL
                   requirements given in this SRS.
                   Once they are complete, you can use your own creativity and
                   imagination to add more features if required.
